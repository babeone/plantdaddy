# Commercial licensing

PlantDaddy is released under the [PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0](LICENSE).
In plain words:

## Free, no permission needed

- Running it for yourself, your family, your flatmates.
- Self-hosting it for a group of friends, for free.
- Studying it, modifying it, publishing your fork under the same license.
- Hobby projects, learning, experimenting, teaching.
- Use by charities, schools, universities, public research bodies, public
  health/safety organisations and government institutions — even when they are
  funded, as the license says explicitly.

## Requires a paid license from me

Anything that makes money, directly or indirectly. Concretely:

- Charging users for access, subscriptions, "pro" tiers or one-off payments.
- Running it as part of a product or service you sell.
- Deploying it inside a company for company purposes.
- **Advertising or any other monetisation** — ad-supported hosting is commercial
  use, even when the app itself is free for the visitor.
- Reselling hosting of it, or bundling it into something you resell.

If your use falls in this list, the noncommercial license does not cover it. Get
in touch and we'll agree terms — the point is not to be difficult, it's that if
the project makes money for someone, the author gets a share.

## How to get one

Open an issue on <https://github.com/babeone/plantdaddy> titled
`Commercial license request`, describing what you want to do with it, or contact
the author through the GitHub profile. Terms are agreed case by case: one-off
fee, revenue share, or a flat yearly license depending on the scale.

## Notes, honestly

- **This is not an OSI-approved open-source license.** PolyForm Noncommercial is
  a source-available license: GitHub will show it as "Other", and some
  organisations refuse noncommercial licenses on principle. That is the
  trade-off for keeping commercial use paid.
- **"Noncommercial" has edge cases.** A freelancer using it to organise a
  client's plants, a nonprofit that also sells merchandise, an
  ad-free-but-sponsored deployment: if you are not sure which side of the line
  you are on, ask before deploying rather than after.
- **This file is an explanation, not the contract.** The binding text is
  [LICENSE](LICENSE). If real money is involved, have a lawyer draft the
  commercial agreement — the author is a developer, not a solicitor, and neither
  is the model that wrote this file.
